Theldurin the Lost lives rough in the Badlands and talks to himself, and some of what he says is worth hearing. He was once a member of the Twilight's Hammer. By the cult's own account he lost his mind, scorned its teachings and fled, taking with him the scroll that could call Myzrael to the surface. Theldurin's account is that he came to his senses. Something waits beneath the earth, the powers his old order calls the dwellers beneath, and he will not say their names in case they hear him.

What he wants now is to understand them well enough to keep them down. He sends travellers into Uldaman for a tablet kept in a chest older than any dwarven work, then for Yagyin's Digest, an old book his former order keeps in many places for its most learned to study. One copy is held in a shop in Ironforge's Forlorn Cavern, by a dwarf named Gerrig Bonegrip who greets the cult's sigil with praise to the dwellers beneath. Another is kept in the Undercity by Keeper Bel'dugur.

I would rather that first copy were anywhere but here. His shop, Bonegrip's Runes and Dooms, has sold books of unpronounceable names and living sacrifices under our king's mountain. Bonegrip, and Zaruk at Hammerfall for the Horde, send travellers after Theldurin with the same words: "our order, the Twilight's Hammer". They call him mad. Having seen what his old order keeps on its shelves, I think he may be the sanest member it ever had. If the dwellers beneath are ever freed, he says, we are all worm food. On that point the Royal Historian and the madman agree.
