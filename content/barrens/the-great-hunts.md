The tauren give the great beasts of the Barrens names, and hunting one is a lesson as much as a trial. Sergra Darkthorn sets travellers on that path at the Crossroads, and Jorn Skyseer at Camp Taurajo and Mahren Skyseer on the coast south of Ratchet take them further along it. I have the account from those who walked it, and from the tauren names themselves, which say more than our names would.

Echeyakee, Whitemist, is king of the savannah cats, so quiet in the hunt that his prey has no time for fear or pain: mercy and death at once, the tauren say. He keeps his lair among the bones of giant kodo north-east of the Crossroads and answers a horn. Ishamuhale, Speartooth, is the fiercest sunscale raptor of the plains, who kills because killing is his nature, and comes to a fresh zhevra left at a dead tree. Lakota'mani, Earthshaker, is a great kodo whose steps are like gathered thunder. Washte Pawne, Spirit Biter, is a thunderhawk whose death may mean great fortune or great doom, and whose feather hurts to hold. Isha Awak, Deep Doom, is lord of the waters off the coast, and the humans on that coast fear him for good reason.

What the hunter brings back is a fang, a hoof, a feather or a heart, and with it, the Skyseers say, something of the beast: the strength in subtlety and the honour in mercy, strength held with temperance, courage, the wisdom that regret becomes, and eyes that will one day learn to see. We dwarves hunt to eat and to boast. I am not sure we have ever thought to ask the boar what it had to teach.
